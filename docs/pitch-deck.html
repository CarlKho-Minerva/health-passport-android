01 / Problem

Medical continuity breaks exactly when care becomes urgent.

Prescriptions live in camera rolls. Lab reports arrive in different formats and languages. Every new clinic asks the same questions again. Cloud-first health AI makes the privacy trade before the user even gets value.

Lived Problem

Globally mobile care is structurally broken.

The product started from a real workflow: crossing cities and countries, then rebuilding a personal health story from memory while stressed, sleep-deprived, or sick.

  • Photos, PDFs, and prescriptions pile up with no usable structure.
  • Doctors need context fast, not a pile of screenshots.
  • Patients end up being the integration layer.

Trust Problem

Health AI should not default to cloud upload.

The highest-value records are also the least appropriate to casually ship through third-party APIs and analytics pipelines.

  • Sensitive records should stay on the user's device by default.
  • Local inference removes the most obvious exposure path.
  • Privacy is part of the product, not a legal afterthought.

Workflow Problem

Extraction alone is not enough.

Scanning a prescription is table stakes. The real job is making records understandable, searchable, and handoff-ready without hallucinating beyond the source material.

  • Grounded Q&A over the user vault.
  • Timeline and medication coherence across files.
  • Clinic-ready summaries when context has to move fast.
Health memory.

This is the company legibility move: not just 'AI scans medical docs,' but a local-first health memory layer that makes medical context portable, queryable, and useful in the moment care happens.

02 / Product

A private health vault with an interface normal people can actually use.

Health Passport accepts documents, voice, and text. It files records into a structured vault, answers grounded questions about those records, and turns the result into something a clinician can use.

Camera -> OCR -> vault Grounded analysis only

Document sample

Real-world inputs are messy. The product still has to feel clean.

Prescriptions, lab reports, receipts, and visit notes get turned into structured health memory instead of staying trapped as dead images.

Core promise

  • Medical document in.
  • Structured local record out.
  • Grounded answer or handoff generated from that record.

Capture

Phone camera, uploaded file, or live voice input. No desktop EHR integration is required to get started.

02

03

Handoff

The system can turn the same records into compact, clinic-ready summaries instead of generic chat transcripts.

Moat

Local by default, structured by design.

  • Markdown-style health vault keeps the record inspectable and portable.
  • Model routing keeps the reasoning layer tied to real sources.
  • System is usable on a phone before any enterprise integration exists.

Performance

  • 446 ms time to first token on Snapdragon 8 Elite.
  • OCR scan time under 1 second in the current stack.
  • Up to 6 local models in the wider routing architecture.

Why now

  • Gemma 4 sharpens the reasoning and handoff layer, not just extraction.
  • On-device inference is finally strong enough to feel product-grade.
  • The user benefit is immediate even before clinical integrations exist.
